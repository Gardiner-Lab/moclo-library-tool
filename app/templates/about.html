What it holds

Parts

Level 0 elements: promoters, coding sequences, terminators, introns and others, each with its 4 bp fusion sites, where it came from and who contributed it.

Cassettes

Level 1 transcription units — promoter, CDS, terminator — assembled from parts and kept with the parts they were made from.

Backbones

The acceptor vectors each level assembles into, with their markers and the enzyme they are opened with.

Plasmids

Assembled Level 1 and Level 2 plasmids, showing every part inside them, exportable as GenBank, FASTA, SnapGene or an image.

CRISPR Designer

Guides and their oligos for a target, with the whole route planned from guide modules up to the final construct.

Saved constructs

On your dashboard: what you planned, with its reactions ready to open in the calculator and a link to the plasmid itself.

The levels

Each level is cut by a different Type IIS enzyme, so building the next one never disturbs the last:

Level 0 parts— BsaI → Level 1 transcription unit— BpiI → Level 2 multigene plasmid

What makes the library work is that the 4 bp fusion sites are standard: a promoter from one person's project drops into another's assembly without anything being redesigned.

What it does for you

  • Checks compatibility before you order or pipette anything: overhangs that clash or leave a gap, and internal BsaI or BpiI sites that the reaction would cut.
  • Assembles in silico, joining the parts through their fusion sites and showing the plasmid you are about to make, with every part marked.
  • Works out the reaction mix, equimolar, scaled to the number of reactions.
  • Designs CRISPR guides and plans the constructs that carry them, making the records as it goes.

With the other tools

  • Plasmid Editor — shares this library. Parts, backbones and plasmids open there for editing, restriction analysis or read alignment; a plasmid there can be contributed back as a Golden Gate part once the editor has checked it.
  • Lab Stocks — confirming an assembled plasmid by sequencing makes its glycerol stock.
  • Lab Notebook — a reaction can be put into a day page with its parts and volumes.
  • Seed Bank — the lines carrying a plasmid, and their seeds and plants.

Who sees what

The parts library is shared by the lab: that is the point of it. Each part keeps its contributor and lab source, so credit and provenance stay with it. Your saved constructs on the dashboard are yours. Everything is kept on your own server and backed up each night with the rest of the suite.
